## A Premium Tier Without the Damascus Branding

Miyabi's Artisan line, built on the 6000MCT model code, prices an 8-inch chef's knife at roughly $230-$285, per the official product page -- above Kaizen II, in the same general range as Birchwood SG2, and below the flagship Black line. What's less obvious from the price alone is that Artisan's official specifications describe a different blade construction than the other lines it's priced alongside: a three-layer clad SG2/MC63 steel core finished with a hand-hammered Tsuchime texture, not the stated multi-layer Damascus pattern used on Kaizen II, Birchwood SG2, and Black.

## What the Steel and Handle Actually Buy

The core steel is SG2 micro-carbide powder steel, branded MC63 by Zwilling, hardened to 62-64 HRC per the product-specific pages checked. That sits between Kaizen II's approximately 61 HRC FC61 steel and Black's 65-66 HRC MC66 steel -- a real, verifiable step up in edge-holding hardness over Kaizen II, without reaching Black's higher-maintenance territory. The handle is Cocobolo Rosewood Pakkawood with brass and red spacers, a mosaic pin, and a stainless-steel end cap -- a more detailed handle build than Kaizen II's plainer Pakkawood composite, per the specifications reviewed.

One inconsistency worth flagging: Miyabi's Artisan collection landing page separately states an approximate 66 HRC hardness figure, which does not match the 62-64 HRC stated on the individual chef's knife and slicing knife product pages. Based on available information, the naming pattern across Miyabi's tiers -- FC61 at roughly 61 HRC, MC63 at roughly 62-64 HRC, MC66 at 65-66 HRC -- suggests the model number is meant to track the target hardness, which is consistent with the lower, product-specific figure rather than the collection page's outlier number. We use 62-64 HRC in the specs above and flag this as an unresolved discrepancy rather than picking a number silently.

## The Manufacturing Process Behind the Price

Per Zwilling's official Japan-market production pages, every Miyabi knife -- not just Artisan -- goes through a heat treatment the manufacturer calls "Fire and Ice," heating the blade to approximately 1000 degrees Celsius before rapidly chilling it to below zero, and a production process the manufacturer states runs to 100 or more individual steps over roughly 42 days per knife. That process cost is baked into Miyabi's pricing across the board, which is part of why even the mid-tier Kaizen II line isn't cheap. It's a useful piece of context for understanding why Miyabi's price floor sits where it does, but it doesn't by itself explain Artisan's premium over Kaizen II -- that premium is specifically the steel hardness and handle material, not a process step unique to this tier. The edge on every tier, Artisan included, gets the same three-stage Honbazuke hand finish -- see our [Miyabi honbazuke sharpening guide](/knives/miyabi-honbazuke-sharpening-guide/) for what that means for home maintenance.

## Where Artisan Sits in Miyabi's Lineup

Artisan occupies a middle-premium position: a clear step up from Kaizen II in steel hardness and handle detail, priced close to Birchwood SG2 but without Birchwood's stated 100-layer Damascus pattern or Karelian birch handle. For a closer look at how Kaizen II and Birchwood specifically compare on steel and handle materials, see our [Kaizen II vs. Birchwood SG2 guide](/knives/miyabi-kaizen-vs-birchwood/). Buyers who want the hardest steel in the US lineup and don't mind the added care that comes with it should look at the flagship Black line instead, covered in our [Miyabi Black 5000MCD67 guide](/knives/miyabi-black-5000mcd67-guide/).

## Bottom Line

Artisan's price is backed by a genuine steel and handle upgrade over Kaizen II, and the manufacturing process behind it -- Fire and Ice heat treatment, 100+ production steps, Honbazuke hand-finishing -- is real, verifiable, and shared across Miyabi's lineup rather than being specific to this tier. What buyers shouldn't assume is that Artisan carries the same layered Damascus look as Kaizen II, Birchwood, or Black -- the official pages describe a hand-hammered Tsuchime finish instead. If the SG2/MC63 steel and Cocobolo Rosewood handle are what you're after, Artisan delivers them at a reasonable middle-tier price. If the Damascus pattern itself is the draw, check the specific listing photos first.
