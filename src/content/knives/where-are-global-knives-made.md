## Global Knives Are Made in Tsubame City

Yoshikin -- the full company name is Yoshida Metal Industry Co., Ltd. -- manufactures all Global knives at its facility in Tsubame City, Niigata Prefecture, Japan. The company was founded on December 27, 1954, per the founding record reviewed on Wikipedia. GLOBAL as a knife brand launched in 1985.

There is no manufacturing ambiguity here: the company, the location, and the founding year are all documented in primary sources. For US buyers verifying authenticity before purchasing, this is the starting point.

## Tsubame City Is Not a Traditional Cutlery Town

Japan has two well-known cutlery manufacturing centers: Seki City in Gifu Prefecture, which is where Shun and Miyabi are made, and Sakai in Osaka Prefecture. Both Seki and Sakai have histories rooted in sword-forging, and their modern kitchen knife industries use forge-and-grind processes derived from that tradition.

Tsubame City's metalworking industry has a different origin. The city's production base developed around Western-style tableware for export -- spoons, forks, and hollow-handle table knives -- with manufacturing techniques built on pressing, polishing, and hollow-body fabrication. Multiple industry sources associate Tsubame with Western-style tableware production, though we did not locate a primary statistical source for the specific production-share figures that appear in trade literature during the scope of our research.

Yoshikin began as part of that tableware tradition. Its 1954 founding product was a hollow-handle table knife for Western export markets -- which is why the Global G-2's hollow sand-weighted handle looks and works the way it does.

## The Construction Follows the Manufacturing Heritage

The one-piece all-stainless construction with a hollow sand-weighted handle is not a design choice Global arrived at arbitrarily. It applies the same hollow-body technique Yoshikin had used for 30 years on tableware before the GLOBAL knife line launched. The technical capability for that construction was already in place.

The practical result is real: no seams between blade and handle, easier sanitation, a forward-balanced feel suited to a pinch grip. Based on the manufacturing lineage reviewed, the edge geometry and hardness profile (Cromova 18 at HRC 56-58 for the G-Series) appear to reflect a tableware fabricator's approach to kitchen knives rather than a forge-ground cutlery tradition.

## Seki City vs. Tsubame City: Different Systems

When comparing Global to Shun or Miyabi, both of which are Seki City products, the comparison involves two different metalworking systems under the same "Made in Japan" label. Seki's forge-and-grind process and the steel specifications it enables (Shun's VG-MAX at HRC 60-61, Miyabi's SG2 at HRC 63+) produce different edge characteristics than Tsubame's one-piece stamped and hollow-ground construction. Neither is inherently superior -- they are different. See our [Miyabi vs. Global Knives guide](/knives/miyabi-vs-global-knives/) for a direct comparison between the two manufacturing traditions.

## Authenticity Verification

Yoshikin has issued an official counterfeit warning about fake Global knives sold on eBay, using names like "GLOBAL Chef" or "GLOBAL Professional" that do not correspond to any real product line. The counterfeit knives appear in branded knife rolls containing multiple pieces.
