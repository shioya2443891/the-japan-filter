## The Price Gap Is Real -- on Official Prices

The G-2's Japan official price of 12,100 yen (roughly $82 at August 2026 exchange rates) against the US official price of $149.95 is a genuine gap, not a rounding error. The US official site price is approximately 83% higher than the Japan official price for the same knife.

That is a larger price premium than most buyers expect, and it is fully confirmed from primary sources -- both prices come directly from the respective official sites.

## The Gap Shrinks on Amazon

The official-price comparison, however, is not necessarily the right baseline. Amazon.com listings for the G-2 ranged from approximately $100 to $140 in prior research (August 2026) -- a range that narrows the Japan gap significantly. At $100, the difference with Japan's $82 is about $18. At $149.95, the difference is $68. Knowing which end of the US range applies to a specific purchase matters more than the headline official-price comparison.

## Warranty Coverage Changes the Math

Whether the gap is $18 or $68, it is partially offset by what a Japan purchase gives up. Based on the warranty and authorized retailer policy documents we reviewed, Global Cutlery USA's warranty does not cover knives purchased outside authorized US channels. A knife bought in Japan and brought to the US would not be covered under those terms.

For a knife intended to last years of regular kitchen use, that warranty gap is a real cost, even if it is not visible on the price tag. Repair or replacement under warranty -- for manufacturing defects, edge retention issues, or handle problems -- would not be available for an unauthorized purchase.

## Where to Buy in the US

The Global Cutlery brand store on Amazon.com is operated by SCANPAN USA Inc., which is Global Cutlery USA's exclusive US distributor. Purchasing from this store is the most straightforward authorized route we identified. Third-party Amazon sellers are subject to SCANPAN's authorized retailer policy, which requires prior written consent -- a lower price from a third-party seller may indicate an unauthorized listing, which would void warranty coverage under the policy we reviewed.
