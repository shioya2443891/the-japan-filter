## A Line With a Different Origin Story

Global's other two main US lines have straightforward histories: the G-Series launched in 1985 as the original design, and SAI followed as a premium tier with harder steel, both sold through SCANPAN USA's exclusive US distribution and through Global's own Japan retail channels, as covered in our [complete Global Knives guide](/knives/best-global-knives-guide/). UKON is different. Both Global Cutlery USA and Williams Sonoma describe it as made in Japan exclusively for Williams Sonoma -- a collaboration product rather than a line Yoshikin sells broadly through its own channels.

## What the Collaboration Actually Changed

UKON keeps Global's core construction -- one-piece stainless, Cromova 18 steel -- but adds a redesigned handle: an ambidextrous thumb rest and a dual-surface grip (smooth on one side, textured on the other). Global's own marketing cites C.A.T.R.A. testing showing the UKON edge as 10% sharper out of the box than its standard grind. What UKON does not change is the steel itself -- it's still Cromova 18 at the G-Series hardness tier, not SAI's harder SANSO steel. The premium over the G-2 is paying for the handle redesign and edge-finishing process, not a metallurgy upgrade.

## Why It Wasn't in the Japan Shop Pages We Checked

While researching Global's other lines, we reviewed two Yoshikin Japan direct-shop category pages that together listed the standard G-Series, the budget GS-series, and the complete GLOBAL-IST lineup -- over 90 products in total. UKON did not appear on either page. Given UKON's confirmed status as a Williams-Sonoma exclusive, this makes sense: it's a product built for a specific US retail partnership, not part of Yoshikin's general Japan retail catalog. That said, our review covered only two online listing pages, not Japan's physical retail network, so this is best read as "not found in the pages we reviewed" rather than proof that UKON is unavailable anywhere in Japan.

## Should You Buy UKON Over the G-2?

If the ergonomic thumb-rest handle is something you'd actually notice and value during regular use, UKON is a reasonable step up from the G-2 at a moderate premium. If you're comparing purely on cutting performance, the two lines share the same steel and hardness, so the G-2 -- with broader availability through SCANPAN USA's standard distribution -- is the more straightforward choice. For a harder-steel upgrade instead of an ergonomic one, see our [SAI series guide](/knives/global-sai-series-guide/).
