## Twenty Years, One Collection

Miyabi IKI is the collection Zwilling built to mark the brand's 20th anniversary, launched in Japan on June 13, 2025. It sits above the existing Black flagship tier in the US lineup, priced from roughly $300 for the paring knife to nearly $500 for the chef's knife or santoku, per the listings we reviewed. The name "iki" translates roughly to refined grace, and the collection is built around a design language distinct from anything else in Miyabi's mainline catalog: a hand-carved handle and a Damascus pattern created specifically for this line rather than reused from an existing tier.

## The Same Steel as an Existing Flagship

Before paying the IKI premium, it's worth knowing what it doesn't buy. IKI's blade uses MC66 (ZDP189) powder steel hardened to approximately 66 HRC -- the same steel type and essentially the same hardness range as Miyabi's existing Black flagship line, per manufacturer specifications on zwilling.com/us. There is no documented harder steel, no different edge geometry, and no separate performance claim distinguishing IKI's cutting ability from Black's. What differs is the Damascus pattern (designed specifically for IKI rather than shared with Black) and the handle, which moves from Black's finished bolster to a hand-carved Maple Burl handle with a glass-coated finish. For a buyer deciding purely on cutting performance, this makes Black -- covered in our [Miyabi Black 5000MCD67 guide](/knives/miyabi-black-5000mcd67-guide/) -- the more cost-efficient way to get the same steel and hardness at a lower price.

## A Handle With a Documented Source

What IKI does add is a specific, traceable design story. Zwilling Japan's press materials describe the handle's carved curves as drawing on the mountains and water of Gifu Prefecture -- the region that includes Seki City, where Miyabi is manufactured -- and describe the Damascus pattern as representing moonlight reflecting on water. The English-language descriptions used on zwilling.com/us and cutleryandmore.com independently echo the same imagery: lakes, cascading water, and moonlight. That the US-facing marketing language lines up with the Japanese press description, without reading like a direct translation of it -- based on available information, this may suggest the design story is the same across both markets rather than invented separately for each. Based on the sources reviewed, it appears to be a more traceable basis for a "made with intention" claim than a purely marketing-invented design story, even though it doesn't change how the knife performs.

## Availability: Confirmed Now, Not Guaranteed Later

At the time of this research (August 2026), the IKI chef's knife, santoku, and paring knife were all listed as in stock on zwilling.com/us and cutleryandmore.com. On Amazon.com, we could confirm a direct listing only for the 3.5-inch paring knife; we did not find a confirmed direct Amazon listing for the chef's knife or santoku within this research scope, which suggests Amazon's coverage of this specific collection is partial rather than complete. Whether IKI continues as an ongoing part of Miyabi's US catalog once the initial production run sells through was not confirmed in our research -- it is documented as a limited anniversary collection, not a standing line with the same restock cadence as Kaizen or Black. Buyers who specifically want this design should treat current in-stock status as a reason to act rather than an assumption that the same piece will be available, at the same price, later.

## Bottom Line

Miyabi IKI is a well-documented 20th-anniversary collection with a design story that traces back to Miyabi's own manufacturing region, and it was confirmed purchasable across multiple channels at the time of this research. But the steel and hardness match Miyabi's existing Black flagship line, so the premium is paying for the handle, the pattern, and the limited-production status -- not a documented performance upgrade. Buy IKI specifically for the anniversary design and act while current stock is confirmed; buy Black instead if the goal is the same cutting performance for less. The collection comes in both a chef's knife (gyuto) and a santoku shape at the same price -- see our [santoku vs. gyuto guide](/knives/miyabi-santoku-vs-gyuto/) if you're unsure which shape fits your kitchen before choosing between them.
