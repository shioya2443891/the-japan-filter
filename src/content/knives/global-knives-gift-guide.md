## The Service Gap Worth Knowing About

If you've read about Yoshikin's Roppongi store -- covered in our guide on [where Global Knives are made](/knives/where-are-global-knives-made/) -- you may already know it offers engraving. What's less obvious is that this service does not appear to have a US equivalent. Global Cutlery USA's gift pages, which we reviewed directly, list pre-configured sets and digital gift cards, but no engraving or complimentary gift-wrapping option. If personalization is the deciding factor in your gift, that's worth knowing before you assume a US purchase can replicate the Japan in-store experience.

## What US Gift-Givers Get Instead

Without a US engraving option, the practical path to a considered-feeling gift is choosing a set that already reads as complete: a boxed knife-and-sharpener combination, or a named multi-piece set, rather than a single loose knife. The G-2220GB pairs the flagship G-2 with a sharpener in a gift box -- functional and presentable without any extra customization. The SAI-3001 set moves the gift up a tier with SAI's harder steel and Komin Yamada's continued design involvement, a detail covered in more depth in our [SAI series guide](/knives/global-sai-series-guide/).

## If You're Buying During a Japan Trip

For buyers who happen to be in Tokyo, the Roppongi flagship store's engraving service is a genuine way to add something that Global's authorized US gift channels do not appear to offer based on the pages we reviewed. The tradeoff is warranty coverage: knives purchased outside SCANPAN USA's authorized US channels are not covered under the US warranty, a point detailed in our guide on [where to buy Global Knives in the US](/knives/global-knives-where-to-buy-us/). Weigh the sentimental value of an engraved, in-person purchase against that loss of warranty coverage before planning a purchase around the trip.

## Choosing Between the Two Sets

For most US gift budgets, the G-2220GB is the safer default -- a well-known knife shape, a bundled sharpener, and a price that doesn't require justifying a splurge. Move up to the SAI-3001 when the recipient already owns a basic chef's knife and would appreciate the edge-retention upgrade that SAI's harder steel provides.
