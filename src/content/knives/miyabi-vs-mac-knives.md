## Two Companies, Same City, Opposite Paths

MAC Professional and Miyabi Evolution are both stamped "Made in Seki City, Japan," and both land in roughly the same price range. That surface similarity hides two very different corporate stories. MAC Corporation is a Japanese company headquartered in Sakai, Osaka Prefecture -- a region with its own centuries-old single-bevel blade tradition -- that has manufactured its knives in Seki City, a separate historic blade region, since 1965. Miyabi is the reverse case: a German company, Zwilling J.A. Henckels, built its own dedicated factory in Seki City specifically to produce Miyabi, rather than making it at Zwilling's home base in Solingen, Germany.

## A Japanese Company Manufacturing Outside Its Own Region

Sakai's blade-making history predates Seki's in some respects -- the region's swordsmithing tradition eventually gave way to single-bevel tobacco knives so respected that the Tokugawa shogunate granted Sakai blacksmiths an official quality seal, according to historical accounts of the region. MAC carries that region's name in its corporate address, but its knives are not Sakai-forged single-bevel blades. Per the company's official history page, MAC's factory has operated in Seki City, Gifu Prefecture since 1965 -- a different city, with a different forging lineage centered on samurai-sword-descended technique rather than Sakai's tobacco-knife-descended single-bevel specialty.

## Steel, Hardness, and Handle: Where the Real Comparison Sits

At this price tier, the two knives are close on paper. MAC's Professional MTH-80 uses a proprietary molybdenum-vanadium stainless steel at approximately 59-61 HRC, per manufacturer and retail specifications, paired with a triple-riveted Pakkawood handle and stainless bolster. Miyabi's Evolution 400FC uses FC61 fine-carbide steel hardened to 61 HRC, per manufacturer specifications, with a glass-fiber reinforced POM handle rather than a wood composite. The hardness difference is marginal. The more noticeable everyday difference is handle material -- Pakkawood is a wood-resin composite with a traditional look and feel, while POM is a fully synthetic plastic that tends to resist moisture absorption better over years of dishwashing exposure, though hand-washing is still recommended for either knife's edge geometry. For a deeper look at how Miyabi's factory finishes its edges by hand, see our [Miyabi honbazuke sharpening guide](/knives/miyabi-honbazuke-sharpening-guide/) -- the same hand-finishing process applies across Miyabi's lineup, including the Evolution line compared here.

## Warranty: Same Requirement, Different Company Behind It

Both brands tie their warranty to authorized-seller purchases. MAC's official warranty page states its 25-year limited warranty covers only knives bought from authorized sellers; Zwilling's published Miyabi warranty terms carry the same restriction for US purchases. The practical buying risk is identical for either brand -- a third-party Amazon seller outside each company's authorized list can void warranty coverage regardless of which knife you choose.

## What This Means When You're Choosing

If you want a knife built around thin, function-first blade geometry, a long-standing US warranty program, and a design history rooted in working kitchens rather than decorative retail, MAC Professional is the more direct match. If you specifically want Zwilling's FC61 steel formulation, a synthetic handle that resists moisture over time, or simply prefer buying into the Miyabi name, the Evolution line gets you there at a comparable price. Neither company's headquarters city -- Sakai for MAC, Solingen for Zwilling -- is, by itself, a reason to prefer one over the other; both knives are made in the same city by companies that arrived there through opposite paths.

## Bottom Line

MAC Professional and Miyabi Evolution are a rare case of two brands matching closely on price, manufacturing city, and even steel hardness, while differing completely in corporate structure -- a Japanese company manufacturing away from its own historic home region, and a German company's dedicated Japan factory. The better buy comes down to blade geometry preference and handle material, not which company's name sounds more authentically Japanese.
