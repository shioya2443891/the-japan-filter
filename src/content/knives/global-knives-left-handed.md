## Why Handedness Matters for Japanese Knives Specifically

Handedness is a bigger consideration for Japanese kitchen knives than for most Western cutlery, because a meaningful share of traditional Japanese blade shapes are single-bevel: ground flat on one face only, producing an asymmetric edge built for one-handed technique. A single-bevel deba or yanagiba made for a right-handed cook does not perform the same way in a left-handed grip -- the bevel geometry works against the cut rather than with it. Buyers researching Japanese knives sometimes assume this applies to the whole category. It doesn't, and Global is one of the clearer examples of why.

## Where Global's Design Comes From

Global's all-stainless, hollow-handle, sand-weighted construction traces back to Yoshikin's founding in 1954 as a manufacturer of Western-style hollow-handle tableware -- forks, spoons, and table knives built for export, not single-bevel professional cutlery. When the Global knife line launched in 1985, it carried that construction logic forward: a one-piece stainless blade and handle, ground double-bevel on both faces. For more on this manufacturing background, see our guide on [where Global Knives are made](/knives/where-are-global-knives-made/).

Because the edge is symmetrical, there's no handedness-dependent geometry to account for. The G-2, UKON, and SAI-01 all cut the same way whether the knife is in a right or left hand.

## The One Place It Gets More Interesting

Global's Japan-only IST line includes deba and yanagiba shapes -- the same forms that are traditionally single-bevel in professional Japanese kitchens. Our research into that line found that Yoshikin manufactures the IST versions of these shapes double-bevel as well, a deliberate choice for home cooks rather than professional single-bevel technique. That means the ambidextrous design isn't limited to the export-focused G-Series -- it runs through Global's entire catalog, Japan-market and US-market lines alike. For more on the IST line and why its blade shapes differ from the G-Series, see our [GLOBAL-IST guide](/knives/global-ist-japan-only-knives/).

## What This Means for a Purchase Decision

If you're left-handed and shopping for a Global knife, hand dominance simply isn't a variable in the decision. Choose based on steel hardness and budget -- G-2 for the standard HRC 56-58 edge at a lower price, SAI-01 for HRC 58-59 and longer edge retention at a premium. Both function identically in either hand.

The one scenario where this doesn't apply: if you specifically want a genuine single-bevel Japanese blade for one-handed professional technique, Global does not make one in any line we reviewed. That's a different category of knife entirely, and a left-handed buyer in that specific case would need to look for a manufacturer that produces left-handed single-bevel blades directly.
