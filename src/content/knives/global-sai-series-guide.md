## SAI vs. G-Series: One Real Difference

The SAI and G-Series share the same manufacturing base (Yoshikin, Tsubame City), the same one-piece all-stainless construction, and the same hollow sand-weighted handle design. What differs is the steel.

G-Series and UKON use Cromova 18 at HRC 56-58. SAI uses Cromova 18 SANSO -- a 3-ply construction with a different inner core alloy -- reaching HRC 58-59. Two points of Rockwell hardness sounds narrow, but in edge-retention terms it is a real difference: the SAI holds an edge through more cutting cycles before it needs resharpening.

The tradeoff is resharpening behavior. At HRC 58-59, the edge is more sensitive to careless technique -- a coarse stone or wrong angle chips or rolls the edge more easily than at HRC 56-58. That is the specific tradeoff buyers need to evaluate.

## The Length Difference Matters

The SAI-01 -- the flagship chef's knife in the SAI line -- is 7.5 inches. The G-2 is 8 inches. Neither is better in absolute terms, but the difference is real in practice. For smaller cutting boards or work on medium produce, 7.5 inches is often easier to maneuver. For larger proteins, bread, or full-length cuts, 8 inches provides more useful blade surface.

The SAI line does not offer an 8-inch chef's knife equivalent. Buyers who prefer the 8-inch format and want the SAI steel upgrade would need to look at the SAI-02 (5.5-inch prep) or SAI-03 (7-inch santoku) for different functions rather than a longer chef's knife.

## The Design Narrative: What It Is and Isn't

The SAI handle's seven black-dot inlays are described by the manufacturer as referencing the seven virtues of Bushido. Komin Yamada, who designed the G-Series in 1985, continued his involvement as designer on the SAI -- a design tenure of over 25 years on the same brand, which is an accurate and unusual fact.

The Bushido narrative is worth understanding clearly: Yoshikin was founded in 1954 in Tsubame City as a hollow-handle tableware manufacturer for Western export markets, not as a cutlery maker in the sword-forging tradition associated with Seki City or Sakai. The seven-dot motif is Yamada's design choice applied to a product from a tableware-origin manufacturer. It is a visual design statement, not a heritage claim about the factory.

That context does not diminish the knife's quality -- the SANSO steel specification and manufacturing are well-established. It does help buyers evaluate the narrative accurately against what the brand actually is.

The SAI-01 (7.5-inch chef's knife) is the clearest starting point in the SAI line -- the most widely available model and the closest functional equivalent to the G-2. The SAI-03 (7-inch santoku) serves the same steel upgrade for buyers who prefer santoku geometry.
