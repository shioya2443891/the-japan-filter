## Where Tiger's US Lineup Actually Sits

Tiger sells four rice cooker technology tiers in Japan: Micom, IH, Pressure IH, and a donabe-inspired flagship. Only the first two -- Micom and IH -- appear in the brand's official US catalog based on the listings reviewed for this guide. That's a real ceiling, not a temporary gap, and it's worth knowing going in rather than discovering after comparing Tiger to a brand that does sell pressure cooking in the US.

## Budget Pick: Micom with Tacook

The JBV-S10U is Tiger's least expensive US model with confirmed Made in Japan manufacturing, and it still includes the Tacook plate -- a raised steaming surface that cooks a side dish using the same heat cycle as the rice below it. Competing brands in the same price range generally don't bundle an equivalent feature at this tier.

## The IH Tier Is the Ceiling

Both JKT-D models step up to induction heating, which reviewers commonly describe as producing more consistent texture across different rice types than Micom heating. Neither model confirmed Tacook inclusion in the listings reviewed, so if synchronized side-dish cooking matters as much as heating precision, check the specific listing before assuming it carries over from the Micom tier.

## Which One to Buy

For most households, the JKT-D10U balances confirmed Made in Japan manufacturing, IH heating precision, and a 5.5-cup capacity that fits typical daily use. Buyers who specifically want Tacook's side-dish feature and are working with a tighter budget should start with the JBV-S10U instead. Larger households should size up to the JKT-D18U rather than running a smaller cooker at capacity.
