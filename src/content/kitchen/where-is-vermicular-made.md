## Aichi Dobby: The Foundry Before Vermicular

Aichi Dobby Corporation was founded in Nagoya in 1936. The company's original business was
iron casting for dobby looms -- components in the machinery used to control weaving patterns
in textile production. Nagoya, in Aichi Prefecture, is a manufacturing center historically
associated with automobiles, ceramics, and precision industrial goods.

Dobby loom manufacturing was a viable business in postwar Japan but became increasingly
difficult as Japan's domestic textile industry declined and overseas manufacturing grew.
By the 2000s, Aichi Dobby was in financial distress.

## How the Company Transitioned

Kunihiro Hijikata, son of the founding family, returned from a career as a foreign exchange
dealer at Toyota Tsusho Corporation to join the family business in 2001. His younger brother
Tomoharu, who had been in cost management at Toyota Motor Corporation, joined in 2006.

The two brothers identified precision machining as the company's transferable technical asset.
Aichi Dobby had developed expertise manufacturing components for hydraulic systems in marine
and construction equipment -- parts where the gap between mating metal surfaces has functional
consequences for how the component performs under pressure. The lid-to-body contact surface
on the Oven Pot applies the same principle to cookware: a tighter fit retains steam more
effectively inside the pot.

Development of the consumer cookware line began in 2007. The first Vermicular Oven Pot launched
in February 2010.

## The Precision Specification

Vermicular states the Oven Pot's lid and body are machined to a contact gap of under 0.01mm.
To calibrate that figure: a standard sheet of printer paper is approximately 0.1mm thick.
The stated Vermicular tolerance is smaller than a tenth of that gap.

This specification is what the company says enables Musui -- waterless cooking -- by trapping
steam that would otherwise escape from a less precise lid fit. The claim is grounded in the
industrial machining background described above, not in a general assertion of quality derived
from the country-of-origin label.

## Where the Products Are Made

Aichi Dobby manufactures Vermicular products at its Nagoya facility. The company has not
outsourced production -- the same foundry that pivoted from textile machinery components to
cookware in 2010 continues to produce the Oven Pot and other products in the Vermicular
lineup. As of August 2026, the company's annual revenue was approximately JPY 3.6 billion
with approximately 251 employees.

The Santoku kitchen knife released in Japan in June 2026 is also manufactured at the same
Nagoya facility. As of August 2026, we did not find it available through US distribution channels in our research.

## What "Made in Japan" Means Here

For most cookware brands, "Made in Japan" is a geographic description that may or may not
correspond to a specific production technique. For Vermicular, the Japan manufacturing origin
is directly connected to the precision specification -- because the facility that makes the
pots is the same facility that developed the machining technique from industrial applications.

US buyers evaluating the "Made in Japan" claim on a Vermicular box can verify it against
the company's published history and the specific tolerance specification stated in the product
documentation. Whether that specification is relevant to their cooking style is the practical
question -- covered in the Oven Pot 2.0 review on this site.
