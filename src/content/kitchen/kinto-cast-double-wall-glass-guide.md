## An Everyday Glass, Repositioned

The gap between how CAST is sold in Japan and how it sometimes appears in US retail contexts is a useful case study in how the same product can carry different market positioning depending on where it's sold. In Japan, the CAST Water Glass is a 605-yen item at Loft or Hands -- comparable in retail context to buying a set of drinking glasses at a well-stocked housewares store. In some US kitchen and gift roundups, the same glass appears framed as a design object.

## What the Double-Wall Construction Actually Does

Per manufacturer marketing, the double-wall design is meant to reduce condensation forming on the glass's exterior and to insulate the drinker's hand from hot or cold contents. This is a common feature across double-wall glassware from multiple brands, not unique to KINTO. This guide did not independently verify insulation performance -- if that specific functional claim matters to your purchase, check current customer review patterns for reported real-world performance.

## Why the Price Gap Is Smaller Than It Looks

Percentage-wise, the roughly 79% premium on CAST looks similar to or larger than the premium on KINTO's more expensive items. In absolute terms, it is about $3 -- a small amount relative to the CLK-151 plates' $8-19 absolute gaps or the Travel Tumbler's roughly $13 gap. For buyers deciding whether the import premium is worth it, CAST is the lowest-stakes decision in KINTO's catalog.

## Who Should Buy This

CAST works well as an everyday glass for water, cold brew coffee, or casual cocktails, and as a low-cost way to evaluate KINTO's design language before committing to a higher-priced item like the Travel Tumbler or a CERAMIC LAB plate. Buyers looking specifically for a Japan-made product should look at CERAMIC LAB instead -- CAST, like the Travel Tumbler, is made in China.
