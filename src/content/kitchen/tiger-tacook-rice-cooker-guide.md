## What Tacook Actually Is

Tacook is a raised plate that sits inside the rice cooker's inner pot, positioned above the water and rice line. As the rice cooks, the same steam and heat that cooks it also cooks whatever's placed on the plate -- typically vegetables, fish, or another simple side dish. It's a mechanical addition to the pot itself, not a separate heating zone or electronic feature.

## Which Tiger Models Include It

Based on the official listings reviewed for this guide, Tacook is confirmed on Tiger's JBV-S Micom series (both the 5.5-cup and 10-cup versions). It was not confirmed in the listings reviewed for Tiger's JKT-D or JPW-H induction heating models -- so stepping up to IH doesn't automatically bring Tacook with it. Check the specific listing rather than assuming.

## Tacook vs a Second Pot (or a Pressure Cooker)

Tacook's advantage over just using a second pot or steamer basket is that it doesn't require a second heat source or extra stovetop attention -- it rides on the rice cooker's existing cycle. Its limitation is that it only steams; it doesn't sear, roast, or otherwise vary the cooking method for the side dish. A pressure cooker like Instant Pot solves a different problem -- combining ingredients into one pot -- rather than keeping them separate.

## Is It Worth Prioritizing?

If a simple steamed side dish alongside rice is a realistic part of your weeknight cooking, Tacook adds real value at a low price -- it's included on Tiger's cheapest confirmed Made in Japan model. If you're more interested in rice texture, pressure cooking, or a side dish with more than steaming as the cooking method, Tacook shouldn't be the deciding factor in choosing Tiger over another brand.
