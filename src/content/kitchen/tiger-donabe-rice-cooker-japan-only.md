## Why This Model Comes Up in US Searches

Tiger's donabe-style flagship sits at the top of its Japan domestic lineup, and buyers who research the brand deeply enough -- past the IH tier sold in the US -- eventually find references to it. Grey-market listings and import forums exist to meet the resulting demand, but that doesn't mean importing one is the right call.

## What the Banko-Ware Inner Pot Actually Is

The JRT-A100's inner pot isn't a coated metal surface -- it's fired clay, produced by Banko-ware artisans in a process that takes roughly three months per pot. That's a genuinely different cooking surface from anything in Tiger's US-available lineup, and it's the specific thing that can't be approximated by buying a higher US tier.

## The Price Comparison Doesn't Favor Importing

Based on the Japan domestic pricing and US IH pricing reviewed for this guide, the JRT-A100's Japan retail price converts to a higher figure than Tiger's top US-available IH model -- before shipping, customs, or currency fluctuation are even factored in. There's no price arbitrage to capture here.

## What to Buy Instead

The JKT-D18U, covered in more detail in our [full Tiger rice cooker guide](/kitchen/best-tiger-rice-cookers), is the practical ceiling for US buyers purchasing through official Tiger channels, with full Tiger Corporation U.S.A. warranty coverage. If pressure-cooked texture control matters more than the clay-pot cooking method itself, Zojirushi's Pressure IH tier is the closest technology available through an official US channel, even though it's a different brand and mechanism.
