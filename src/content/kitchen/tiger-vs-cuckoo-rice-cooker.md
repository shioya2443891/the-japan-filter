## Different Problems, Different Engineering

It's tempting to treat "Tiger or Cuckoo" as a coin flip between two similarly positioned Asian rice cooker brands. Based on the official product listings reviewed for this guide, that's not quite accurate -- the two companies emphasize different things in their US lineups, and that shows up as genuinely different feature priorities rather than just different price tags on similar hardware.

## Where Each Brand's US Lineup Tops Out

Tiger's official US catalog tops out at induction heating, per the listings reviewed -- Pressure IH exists in Tiger's Japan-market lineup but hasn't appeared through official US channels in the sources checked. Cuckoo sells Pressure IH ("Twin Pressure") directly in the US, with switchable pressure and non-pressure settings on the models reviewed.

## Made in Korea vs Made in Japan

Both brands confirm country of manufacture explicitly in the official listings checked for this guide -- Cuckoo's Twin Pressure models state Made in Korea, and Tiger's Micom and IH models reviewed here state Made in Japan. Neither claim is in question; they're simply different countries of origin for two companies competing in the same US rice cooker category.

## Which One Fits How You Cook

If pressure-cooked rice texture is what you're after, Cuckoo is the model here that delivers it through an official US channel. If a rice-plus-side-dish one-pot meal matters more, Tiger's Tacook-equipped Micom tier gets there at a lower price -- without the pressure-cooking texture control Cuckoo offers.
