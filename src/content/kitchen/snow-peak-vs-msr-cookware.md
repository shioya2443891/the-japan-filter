## Where the Comparison Actually Works

Stoves are the clean comparison. Snow Peak's GigaPower 2.0 and MSR's PocketRocket 2 are both compact, single-burner canister stoves in a similar price range ($39.95-49.95 for the GigaPower depending on the collection page, approximately $49.95 for the PocketRocket 2). If you're choosing between the two brands specifically for a stove, this is a genuine head-to-head, and the decision comes down to specifics like ignition type and packed size rather than a meaningful price gap.

## Where It Doesn't

Cookware is a different story. Snow Peak's Multi Compact Titanium Cookset (SCS-020T, $99.95) is a nesting system: two stacking pots and a frying pan lid, built for cooking for one or two people. MSR's closest titanium product, the Titan Kettle ($64.95), is a single 900ml vessel -- it functions as a pot, mug, or bowl, but it's fundamentally a solo-use item, not a multi-piece cooking system. Comparing the two on price alone (as though the cheaper option is simply the better deal) misses that they're not solving the same problem.

## How to Actually Choose

If you're cooking for one and want the lightest possible single vessel, MSR's Titan Kettle is a legitimate, well-regarded option in its own category. If you're cooking for one or two and want an actual multi-piece cookware system -- a pot for boiling and a separate pan for frying, nested together for pack size -- Snow Peak's SCS-020T is built for that specific use case in a way the MSR Titan Kettle is not designed to match.
