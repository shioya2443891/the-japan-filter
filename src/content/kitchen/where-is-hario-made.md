## Two Different Claims, Often Treated as One

"Hario is a Japanese company" and "this specific Hario product is manufactured in Japan" are related but separate facts. The first is well documented -- Hario was founded in Tokyo in 1921 and has never been anything but a Japanese company. The second depends on which product, and sometimes which component of that product, you're looking at.

## What's Specifically Confirmed

Based on the official product pages reviewed for this guide, the V60 Ceramic Dripper's production region is stated directly: Arita, in Saga Prefecture, a porcelain-producing area with a documented history of more than 400 years. The V60 Glass Dripper's heatproof glass is produced at Hario's own factory in Koga City, Ibaraki Prefecture, which public sourcing information identifies as the only mass-production heatproof glass factory in Japan. Both of these are more specific than a generic "Made in Japan" label -- they name a facility or a region.

## Where It Gets More Complicated

The ChaCha Kyusu Maru teapot is the clearest example of a mixed-origin product in Hario's catalog: the manufacturer's own product page states that the glass body and lid are made in Japan, while the strainer is made in China. This isn't hidden or ambiguous -- it's disclosed directly -- but it does mean "Hario product" and "entirely Japan-made product" aren't interchangeable claims for every item.

The plastic V60 dripper, which is also the most widely purchased and least expensive version, did not carry an explicit country-of-manufacture statement in the listings reviewed for this guide. That's not evidence it's made outside Japan. It means the specific claim wasn't found within this research's scope, and buyers who want certainty should check the individual listing or contact the seller directly.

## A Practical Rule for Checking Any Hario Product

1. Read the full product title and description on the official Amazon listing, not just the search result snippet.
2. Look for a named region or facility (like Arita or Koga City) rather than a generic country label -- Hario's own pages sometimes provide this level of detail.
3. For products with multiple components (glass bodies, strainers, lids), check whether the listing specifies origin per component, as it does for the ChaCha Kyusu Maru.
4. If the listing doesn't state it and it matters to your purchase, contact the seller before buying rather than assuming based on the brand name.
