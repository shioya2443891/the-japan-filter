## A Brewing Method Older Than the V60

Hario's coffee siphon line predates the V60 by more than 50 years: the company first produced a siphon brewer in 1948, while the V60 didn't launch until 2005. For buyers who know Hario primarily through the V60's cone shape, the siphon line represents a much older part of the company's coffee equipment history.

## What the Method Actually Involves

Siphon brewing uses vapor pressure: water in a lower glass chamber is heated until pressure pushes it up into an upper chamber containing the coffee grounds. When the heat source is removed, the brewed coffee is pulled back down through a cloth filter into the lower chamber. It's a genuinely different mechanism than gravity-fed pour-over or a cold steep -- it requires a working heat source, careful handling of two glass chambers, and enough practice to time the process consistently.

## The Kissaten Connection

Multiple specialty coffee and coffee-industry sources describe siphon brewing's historical role in Japan's kissaten -- traditional coffeehouses where the visible, deliberate brewing process was itself part of what customers were served, not just a means to an end. That context is useful for understanding what you're buying: a siphon isn't a faster or more efficient way to make coffee than a dripper. It's a slower, more theatrical one, and that's the point for people who choose it.

## The Part Buyers Often Miss: Consumables

Unlike the V60's paper filters, which are cheap, widely available, and treated as a single-use commodity, the siphon's cloth filter is a reusable component that still needs periodic replacement. Before buying into the siphon method, it's worth confirming that replacement cloth filters are currently available on Amazon.com -- a gap in consumable availability affects long-term usability in a way it wouldn't for a dripper using standard paper filters that are sold by dozens of third parties.

## Who This Is Actually For

If the appeal of siphon coffee is the process as much as the finished cup -- the visible brewing, the ritual, the connection to a specific coffeehouse tradition -- Hario's Technica line is a well-established way to get into it, backed by more than 75 years of the company making this exact style of equipment. If you want a low-maintenance daily brewing method, the V60 or Mizudashi lines covered elsewhere on this site involve less equipment, less risk, and fewer ongoing parts to track down.
