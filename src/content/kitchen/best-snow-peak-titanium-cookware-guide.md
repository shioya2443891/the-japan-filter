## Where to Start

Snow Peak's titanium catalog is wide enough that a first-time buyer can end up comparing a $29.95 mug against a $99.95 cookset without a clear sense of what separates them. The short version: mugs are drinkware, cooksets are for actual cooking, and the size of the cookset should match how many people you're regularly cooking for.

## Cookware: Solo vs. Two-Person

The Multi Compact Cookset (SCS-020T) nests two nesting pots with a frying pan lid -- the most flexible option for one or two people. The Mini Solo Combo (SCS-004TR) trims this down to a single pot and cup built specifically for solo, ultralight trips. If you're regularly cooking for more than two, neither set alone is sufficient -- Snow Peak's larger Trek and Multi Compact system pieces (not covered in depth here) extend the same nesting format further.

## Mugs: Single-Wall vs. Double-Wall

The Ti-Single 450 Cup is the least expensive item in the lineup and the simplest way to try Snow Peak titanium before committing to cookware pricing. The Ti-Double 450 Mug adds double-wall insulation for about $20 more, which keeps drinks warmer or cooler longer -- worth the upgrade if the mug is going to be a daily-use item rather than an occasional camping accessory.

## A Note on Titanium as a Cooking Material

Titanium heats and cools faster than aluminum or stainless steel cookware, which is part of why it's popular for ultralight backpacking -- it's also lighter per unit of strength. The tradeoff reviewers commonly describe is that titanium's fast heat response makes it easier to scorch food, particularly rice or anything with sugar content, if left unattended on a high flame. This is a property of the material, not a defect specific to Snow Peak's construction.
